

**What is AI**  
AI is the branch of computer science focused on building systems that perform tasks normally requiring human intelligence — learning, reasoning, problem-solving, perception, language understanding, and decision-making — by simulating human cognitive functions.

**History & Evolution**  
Traces AI from its 1940s–50s foundations through Turing's Test (1950), the 1956 Dartmouth Conference (birth of the term "AI"), an AI Winter (1970s–80s), the rise of Expert Systems (1980s), Deep Blue beating Kasparov (1997), the deep learning boom (2012), AlphaGo (2016), and the current era of generative AI/LLMs.

**Why AI is Needed**  
Automation and efficiency, big data analysis, personalization (recommendations, healthcare), innovation/research, safety/security, better decision-making, and accessibility (assistive tech, translation).

**Characteristics of AI**  
Learning, reasoning, problem-solving, knowledge representation, perception, NLP, decision-making, adaptability, automation, and self-correction.

**Goals & Applications**  
10 broad goals (problem-solving, healthcare, creativity, ML/deep learning, robotics, planning, AGI, continuous learning, social intelligence, knowledge representation), applied across healthcare, education, finance, transportation, manufacturing, retail, agriculture, cybersecurity, entertainment, and customer service.

**Agents & Environment**  
An AI system = agent + environment. The agent perceives via sensors, processes info, and acts via actuators/effectors in a continuous perceive-think-act cycle.

**AI vs. Robot**  
AI is "programmed to think" (social interaction, learning); a robot is "programmed to do" (low-level interaction, only as smart as its program).

**The AI Problems**  
Tasks are grouped into:

- _Mundane tasks_ (perception, natural language, commonsense reasoning, robot control)
- _Formal tasks_ (games like chess/Go, mathematics)
- _Expert tasks_ (engineering, scientific/medical/financial analysis)  
    Key open questions: assumptions about intelligence, useful techniques, how deeply to model human intelligence, and how to know success.

**AI Technique**  
A good AI technique represents knowledge that generalizes, is understandable, modifiable, usable even if incomplete, and helps narrow down possibilities.

**Machine Learning (ML)**  
A subset of AI where systems learn patterns from data to make predictions without explicit programming. Important for automation, handling big data, accuracy, personalization, and real-time predictions. Applications: spam filtering, recommendations, fraud detection, medical diagnosis, image/speech recognition.

**Neural Networks**  
Modeled on the brain, made of input, hidden, and output layers of neurons using weights, biases, and activation functions (ReLU, Sigmoid, Tanh, Softmax). Trained via backpropagation. Advantages include learning complex/non-linear patterns, high accuracy, automatic feature learning, and scalability. Used in image/speech recognition, NLP, fraud detection, autonomous vehicles.

**Natural Language Processing (NLP)**  
Enables computers to understand/generate human language. Pipeline: text input → preprocessing → feature extraction → model processing → output generation. Key tasks: tokenization, stop-word removal, stemming, lemmatization, POS tagging, Named Entity Recognition, parsing, sentiment analysis, text classification, and summarization. Applications include chatbots, translation, spam detection, and search engines.
